# Dracotype

| “ | In this way, our original monsters have beecome our most powerful. | ” |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | — The Book of Butcher |  |

**Dracotypes** are the most dangerous breed of monster fought by The Order of St. George. Extremely dangerous and highly deadly, it can take an entire House to bring them down, and the killers of dragons are renowned and respected as Emerald Masks and House Dragons. The founder of the Order, St. George himself, created the order with the blood and body of the dragon he slayed.

## History

| “ | Dracotypes are not born of a lone story but a sundry of unspeakable, existential terrors taking shape. A dragon, then, is not a singular fear but a civilization's collective horror. | ” |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | — The Book of Butcher |  |

The Dracotypes, commonly referred to as dragons, have no specific origin myth, which makes their killing incredibly difficult. It seems most likely that Dracotypes, rather than being born of one fear, are made from the fears of the collective. When many existential fears collide, they become a dangerous Dragon.

The founder of the Order, St. Georges himself, slew a dragon at the inception of the organization, and wrote its creed in the dragon's blood. Originally, any aspiring House Dragon would have to kill a dragon to prove their worth; in later years, this transitioned into slaying more metaphorical dragons. Due to the efforts of the Order of St. George, draconic populations reached an all-time low by the 21st century.

Though the Order claims this is disproven, theories persist that any monster could become powerful enough to evolve into a Dracotype. More specifically, many believe that Oscuratypes left too long to fester will become the modern-day dragons, as formless fear taking its rightful shape.

## Description

Hunting Dragons is a difficult feat, one that takes entire Houses and branches of the Order. Emerald Mask hunters are trained to hunt or lead the attacks against them. They exist in many shapes and forms, across multiple cultures.
